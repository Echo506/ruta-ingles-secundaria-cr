Simulacro de comprensión lectora

Leé los tres textos y respondé las 15 preguntas. Usá estrategias de lectura: identificá idea principal, detalles, vocabulario en contexto e información implícita.

Tiempo restante 25:00

Instrucciones

  • Tenés 25 minutos para completar el simulacro.
  • Seleccioná una respuesta por pregunta.
  • Leé con atención: algunas preguntas piden inferencias, no solo detalles directos.
  • Al finalizar, presioná “Finalizar simulacro” para recibir tu resultado.
  • El contenido es práctica original y no reproduce una prueba oficial.
Reading 1

A Library Beyond Books

In a small town in Costa Rica, the public library has changed the way many young people learn after school. For years, students visited the building mainly to borrow novels or use the quiet study area. Today, however, the library also offers workshops on digital skills, creative writing, and community projects.

One of its most popular activities is the “Repair and Share” program. Teenagers bring old electronic devices to the library, where local volunteers teach them how to identify simple problems. Devices that can be repaired are later donated to families who need access to online educational materials. The program does not solve every technological problem in the community, but it has helped participants understand that useful resources do not always need to be new.

Library staff say that the greatest result is not the number of repaired devices. Instead, they value the confidence students gain when they work together, ask questions, and discover practical solutions.

Reading 2

The School Garden Project

At first, the garden behind Central High School was only an empty area with dry soil and broken benches. A group of students proposed transforming it into a place where classes could learn about food, science, and environmental responsibility. The principal supported the idea, but the school did not have enough money to hire a company to do all the work.

Instead of giving up, students contacted parents, local farmers, and former graduates. Some donated seeds and tools, while others offered advice about growing vegetables in the local climate. Science students measured soil conditions, art students painted signs, and mathematics classes helped calculate the amount of water needed each week.

Six months later, the garden produces lettuce, herbs, and tomatoes. A small part of the harvest is used in the school cafeteria, and the rest is shared with a nearby community center. More importantly, the project has shown students that a complex problem can become manageable when different people contribute their knowledge.

6. Why did students contact people outside the school?
7. Which group helped calculate water needs?
8. What does “harvest” refer to in the text?
9. What is the most important lesson of the project?
10. Which statement is true according to the text?
Reading 3

A Message from the Future Careers Club

Dear students,

The Future Careers Club is inviting eleventh-grade students to take part in a series of workshops during the next two months. The sessions will focus on writing a résumé, preparing for interviews, and exploring training opportunities after graduation. Participants will also meet professionals from different fields, including health, technology, tourism, and environmental science.

The club understands that students have different plans. Some may want to enter university, while others may prefer technical programs or begin working. For this reason, the workshops will not recommend one path as the best choice for everyone. Instead, they will provide information and activities to help students identify their strengths, interests, and next steps.

To register, complete the online form before May 20. Students who attend at least four sessions will receive a participation certificate. If you have questions, contact the school counselor by email.

11. What is the purpose of this message?
12. Which topic will NOT be part of the workshops?
13. Why will the club not recommend one path for everyone?
14. What must students do to receive a participation certificate?
15. What does “identify their strengths” mean in this context?
